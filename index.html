Research Interests

I am broadly interested in robot learning, with a focus on reinforcement learning and robot foundation models (WAM, VLA, BFM) for generalizable and long-horizon robot decision-making and control.

My goal is to develop robots that can persistently learn from experience, understand the physical world, and make reliable long-horizon decisions. Although recent multimodal foundation models have shown striking capabilities in multimodal reasoning for robot control, I believe there is still a long way to go before this goal can be achieved.

  • Firstly, despite their extraordinary multimodal reasoning capabilities, they lack a precise understanding of how the physical world evolves. We therefore need to train foundation models that can reason about the consequences of specific actions and choose reasonable next actions.
  • Secondly, multimodal foundation models are good at reading the world, but often fail to generate robust, execution-ready behaviors across different robot morphologies and under challenging real-world scenarios. We need to train generalizable Behavior Foundation Models that can translate multimodal intents into robust, high-frequency robot control signals during real-world deployment.
  • Thirdly, although scaling laws apply to both the multimodal brain and behavior cerebellum, out-of-distribution problems remain inevitable in an ever-changing real world. We therefore need to develop sample-efficient Reinforcement Learning algorithms or self-improvement mechanisms for rapid real-world adaptation.

Ideal Case: a predictive brain proposes and evaluates action intent; a robust cerebellum grounds it into plausible physical behavior; an efficient learning mechanism continuously adapts both to new environments.

CASC: Action Chunking Reinforcement Learning with a Single-action Critic

Dwait Bhatt*, Yifan Xu*, Luobin Wang, Henrik I Christensen, and Nikolay Atanasov

* Equal contribution

Proposed an action-chunking RL algorithm that uses a single-action critic instead of a chunked critic. The algorithm also adapts the executed chunk size based on task stages, and introduces a unified consistent framework for execution, critic bootstrapping, and actor updates.

A Frequency-Domain Performance Evaluation Framework of Quadratic Programming-based Controllers in Motion Capture Measurement System

Yuanchen Tang, Yuxuan Zhao, Yifan Xu, Yongyuan Xu, Ping Feng, Jindi Zhang, and Liang He
