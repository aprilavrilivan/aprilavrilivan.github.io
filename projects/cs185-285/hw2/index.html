Homework 2 · Policy Gradients

REINFORCE, Baselines, and Generalized Advantage Estimation

I implemented a policy-gradient agent and used controlled experiments to study how reward-to-go, neural baselines, advantage normalization, and GAE affect learning stability and sample efficiency.

Overview

Reducing variance in on-policy learning

I began with vanilla REINFORCE and added reward-to-go, a learned value baseline, and normalized advantages. I then implemented generalized advantage estimation and varied its λ parameter to examine the bias–variance tradeoff on longer-horizon tasks.

Experiments

What I evaluated

  • Vanilla and reward-to-go estimators on CartPole.
  • Learned baselines and advantage normalization for variance reduction.
  • Policy-gradient learning on continuous-control HalfCheetah.
  • Multiple GAE λ values on LunarLander.
  • Hyperparameter tuning on InvertedPendulum.

Results

The right estimator and scale mattered

Reward-to-go and a learned baseline generally improved the quality of the gradient estimate, while GAE offered a practical control over temporal credit assignment. With tuned settings, the InvertedPendulum policy consistently reached the task's maximum episode return.
