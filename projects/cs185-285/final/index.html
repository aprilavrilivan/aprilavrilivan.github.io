Calibrated Reward Ensembles for Open-Domain RLHF

I built an end-to-end RLHF pipeline for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, covering offline preference optimization, reward modeling, and online RL. My main investigation used calibrated reward-model ensembles to make candidate selection more reliable across prompts and training methods.

  • DPO · IPO · AOT
  • Reward Modeling
  • GRPO · DrGRPO · GSPO
  • Calibration
  • Ensembling

Overview

A complete alignment pipeline under a fixed benchmark

I worked with a 5,000-example preference benchmark derived from filtered WildChat prompts and compared six policy-optimization approaches against the same frozen base model. The offline branch covered DPO, IPO, and AOT; the online branch combined a Bradley–Terry reward model with GRPO, DrGRPO, and GSPO.

The primary evaluation used head-to-head, judge-based win rate. This made reward reliability central to the project: even a strong policy objective can be undermined when reward models disagree in scale or generalization behavior.

Main Investigation

Calibrate first, then ensemble

I trained three reward models with different random seeds, selected their checkpoints using held-out preference performance, and calibrated their score scales before aggregation. I then used the ensemble to rerank fixed candidates produced by DPO, IPO, and AOT for each prompt.

  • Per-model calibration prevents a reward model with a larger numeric scale from dominating the ensemble.
  • Reusing fixed candidates isolates the quality of reward-based selection from differences in sampling.
  • Repeated judge evaluations measure whether the selected method is robust to evaluator variance.

Ablation

Rank-advantage online RL

I also tested a Rank-Advantage GRPO variant that replaced scalar reward-derived advantages with fixed within-group ranks. This created a simple, scale-invariant online signal. Its strongest configuration was promising, but less stable and ultimately weaker than the calibrated offline reranking method.

Results

The calibrated ensemble gave the strongest result

The selected reward model correctly ordered 216 of 256 held-out preference pairs. Across three evaluator runs, calibrated ensemble reranking achieved win rates of 0.8636, 0.8621, and 0.8571, for an average of 0.8609 against the frozen base model.

0.8609 Average win rate for calibrated ensemble reranking
0.8438 Held-out reward-model accuracy, 216 / 256 pairs
0.8033 Win rate in a saved standard-GRPO evaluation
0.7533 Best Rank-Advantage GRPO average in the ablation
